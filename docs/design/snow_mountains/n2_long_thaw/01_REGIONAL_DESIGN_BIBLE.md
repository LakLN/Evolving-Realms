# The Long Thaw — regional design bible

Status: `PROPOSED_STUDIO_N2`. Nothing in this folder is implemented canon or bound to a World.

## Identity

The Long Thaw is a lower mountain basin where winter does not disappear; it repeatedly loses ground. Snowmelt enters through steep northern fans, divides into braided channels, sinks beneath gravel shelves, and returns as cold springs farther down-valley. A short green season creates reed beds, alder islands, mudflats and grass terraces amid permanent shaded ice.

The subregion is about reading water rather than conquering height. A route safe at dawn may be cut by afternoon melt. Dry stonework records former channels. Animal paths often remain more reliable than roads. The Long Thaw is one local land within the much larger Snow Mountains, not the remainder of the region.

The working name is retained because residents use “long thaw” for both the season and the basin-wide labor of keeping water moving. It does not imply a global climate event.

## Geographic structure

Seven seamless outdoor areas form a looped basin rather than a linear climb:

| `long_thaw:area/alderwash` | Alderwash | Flooded alder islands and root causeways | Thawgate; Braided Commons; Underflow Gorge |
| `long_thaw:area/braided_commons` | Braided Commons | Open seasonal grass and multiple ford choices | Alderwash; Siltmarket; Drowned Orchard |
| `long_thaw:area/siltmarket_reach` | Siltmarket Reach | Seasonal exchange ground above the ordinary flood line | Commons; Nine Weirs; southern regional corridor |
| `long_thaw:area/nine_weirs` | Nine Weirs | Stone waterworks controlling several channels | Thawgate high walk; Siltmarket; Orchard |
| `long_thaw:area/drowned_orchard` | Drowned Orchard | Dead terraces, surviving graft trees and buried walls | Commons; Weirs; Underflow Gorge |
| `long_thaw:area/underflow_gorge` | Underflow Gorge | Water disappears beneath talus and resurges; dangerous alternate loop | Orchard; Alderwash; optional gatehouse interior |

The main low circuit is Thawgate → Alderwash → Commons → Siltmarket → Nine Weirs → Thawgate. Orchard and Underflow form a discovery-heavy inner loop. At least two routes must connect every ordinary outdoor area; seasonal hazards may make a route costly but must not silently strand the player.

Interiors are optional scene boundaries: the dry gatehouse under Weir Five, a seed cellar beneath the orchard terraces, and shallow underflow galleries. Their entrances must be authored landmarks, not generic cave doors.

## Local history

The oldest visible work is a set of terrace walls and nine independent stone weirs. They were built and repaired across generations; tool marks and mismatched masonry deliberately prevent attribution to one lost empire. Later households cultivated hardy graft trees, grazed reedhorn herds and kept flood marks on upright stones.

The basin’s permanent hamlets were abandoned after a sequence of high thaws changed the main channel. Their descendants did not vanish: several family groups now return seasonally to exchange salt, wool, preserved fish, seed and repair labor at Siltmarket. The market is a recurring camp, not a city. Winter leaves it roofless, tied down and mostly empty.

The waterworks are not magic climate controls. They redistribute ordinary meltwater. Their failure can flood a terrace, dry a spawning reach or expose an old route, but cannot end Snow Mountain winter.

## Habitation and social structure

Three overlapping communities are proposed:

- **The Reedbound households** move with herds and fish runs. They own tools and obligations, not fixed borders.
- **The Open-Hand crews** are seasonal weir workers assembled from several households. “Open Hand” is a labor compact, not a state or priesthood.
- **The Upland Remnants** descend from families displaced from the orchard terraces. The label is contested and should appear through documents and individual identities rather than uniform faction costumes.

No group is wholly correct, hostile or culturally uniform. Siltmarket can be empty, preparing, active or breaking camp depending on future seasonal capability. Initial implementation should use environmental evidence and world events; conversational NPCs are a later gated layer.

## Environmental rules

- Meltwater follows authored channels; it is not a universal fluid simulation.
- Fast water is a route hazard, not decorative floor. Every hazardous crossing needs readable banks and an alternate path.
- Cold water increases exposure pressure; warm springs are local exceptions and do not imply the entire basin is geothermal.
- Afternoon/high-thaw states may alter encounter tables and ford availability only after deterministic timing support exists.
- Shelters block exposure but never grant combat immunity.
- Weir state is per Run. World geography and other Runs remain unchanged.

## Narrative themes

The basin asks who bears the cost of “saving” a route or harvest, how communal infrastructure outlives the agreements that maintained it, and whether evidence should be used to restore an old order or design a smaller, fairer compromise. History is discoverable through water levels, repairs, seed records and contradictory flood marks—not a single exposition character.

## Relationship to Hushed Crown

The two clusters share the greater Snow Mountains and evidence of long-distance travel, but The Long Thaw does not resolve the White Choir, hollow carriers, Ysolde, frost runes or the first relay builders. A carrier-mark variant may establish later travellers passing through only after an actual connecting corridor is authored. Existing Hushed Crown rewards and states are neither prerequisites nor rewritten.

## Art and audio narrative

Visual identity: blue-grey gravel, dark alder roots, brief yellow-green grass, milky channels, silt-red repair stakes and low dry-stone works. Buildings are raised, dismantlable and visibly adapted to flooding. Avoid permanent snow-city silhouettes, endless beacon motifs and copied Broken Relay architecture.

Audio should make water direction readable: broad water, under-stone rumble, reed chatter and sudden quiet above blocked channels. Any audio clue must have a visual fallback.
